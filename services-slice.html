The services slice: Why IT services is getting a shrinking share of the AI spending boom

Gartner now expects worldwide IT spending to grow 15.0 percent in 2027, yet it forecasts services spending to grow less than a third as fast, and TCS, Infosys, HCLTech, and Wipro grew more slowly still in their latest quarter. For these providers, the binding constraint may be less the demand cycle than where the new technology dollar is being spent.

by Gaurav Kumar · October 2026

Technology budgets are expanding faster than Gartner expected a year ago. In its October 2026 update, Gartner projected worldwide IT spending of $6.391 trillion in 2026, up 14.5 percent, and $7.352 trillion in 2027, up 15.0 percent. A year earlier, its forecast for 2026 growth was 9.8 percent.

For India's IT services industry, that should be good news. The quarter just reported says otherwise. TCS, Infosys, HCLTech, and Wipro grew revenue between 0.6 percent and 2.8 percent year on year in constant currency in the quarter ended September 30, 2026, and Infosys cut its full-year outlook. This article argues that the gap is structural: the incremental IT dollar is going overwhelmingly to data center hardware and software, and services is capturing a shrinking share of a growing budget. That is an interpretation of forecasts and one quarter of results, and the indicators at the end are the test of it.

Gartner expects services to capture less than 8 cents of each new IT dollar in 2027

Gartner's forecast shows how unevenly the growth is distributed (Exhibit 1). Spending on data center systems is expected to rise 73.8 percent in 2026 and 55.5 percent in 2027. Software is expected to grow 15.2 percent and 14.6 percent. Services, the segment in which Indian providers compete, is expected to grow 4.4 percent and 4.9 percent.

Of the $961 billion that Gartner expects total spending to add in 2027, by this article's calculation, $487 billion goes to data center systems and $76 billion to services. Services' share of total IT spending falls from 24.4 percent in 2026 to 22.2 percent in 2027.

The direction of revisions matters as much as the levels. Between its July and October 2026 updates, Gartner raised its 2026 growth forecast for data center systems from 62.5 percent to 73.8 percent and lowered its forecast for services from 5.3 percent to 4.4 percent, while total growth edged up from 14.2 percent to 14.5 percent. Some of the hardware surge likely reflects price rather than volume: Gartner notes that memory prices have more than doubled over the past 12 months. Even so, the pattern is consistent with AI infrastructure absorbing budget that might otherwise have gone to services. That substitution is this article's reading of the data, not a finding Gartner states.

India's largest providers are growing more slowly than the services segment itself

India's largest providers are not keeping pace even with Gartner's subdued services forecast. In the quarter ended September 30, 2026, constant currency revenue growth year on year was 2.8 percent at TCS, 2.6 percent at HCLTech (4.0 percent for its services business), 1.5 percent at Infosys, and 0.6 percent for Wipro's IT services segment. The comparison is not exact: Gartner's figures are full-year forecasts in current US dollars, and the company figures are single quarters in constant currency. The direction, however, is consistent: measured in reported US dollars, all four also grew less than 4.4 percent.

Forward signals are mixed rather than strong. Infosys revised its fiscal 2027 growth guidance to 1 percent to 2 percent, from 1.5 percent to 3 percent set in July, even as it reported large deal total contract value of $5.2 billion, 62 percent of it net new. Wipro guided to a sequential change of minus 1.5 percent to plus 0.5 percent for its IT services revenue in the December quarter, and its large deal bookings fell 58.6 percent from the prior quarter in constant currency. HCLTech is the exception, guiding to services growth of 3.0 percent to 4.0 percent for the year and adding a net 6,450 people in the quarter.

Demand is not absent. In Gartner's 2027 CEO survey of 203 chief executives and senior business leaders, conducted in April and May 2026, 75 percent planned to increase technology investment, while 44 percent said they struggle to show financial returns from AI. In this article's view, that combination favors spending on infrastructure and platforms that promise future capability over services contracts that buyers now expect to cost less. Bain reports that clients seek efficiency gains of up to 20 percent at renewal and price new work on the assumption of delivery costs at least 30 percent lower.

AI revenue is growing fast, but from a base too small to offset the shift

Providers' own AI businesses are expanding quickly. Infosys said its AI revenue was at 10.5 percent, which this article reads as a share of total revenue, and grew 29 percent from the prior quarter. HCLTech reported advanced AI revenue of $219 million, up 78.0 percent year on year in constant currency, or about 5.9 percent of its $3,724 million in quarterly revenue by this article's calculation. Neither has yet lifted company growth above low single digits. In this article's view, that suggests some AI services revenue may be replacing existing work rather than adding to it.

The more telling move may be TCS's decision to put capital into the fastest-growing segment directly. In November 2025, TCS and TPG agreed to invest up to Rs 18,000 crore over several years, subject to approvals, in HyperVault, a TCS AI data center business targeting more than one gigawatt of capacity. In this article's view, that is a bet that services firms must own part of the infrastructure layer, not only advise on it.

What this means for provider leadership

Leadership teams can consider four actions.

  1. Follow the capital spending. Build design, build, and operate offerings for AI data centers and GPU infrastructure, where Gartner expects half of 2027's spending growth.

  2. Attach to software growth. Treat platform implementation and agent operations on software growing near 15 percent as a core line of business, rather than a partner channel.

  3. Reprice before renewal. Move commoditized service lines to outcome-based or fixed-price terms before clients impose the efficiency gains Bain describes.

  4. Disclose AI revenue on a common basis. Report AI revenue as both an absolute figure and a share of revenue, with clear definitions, so investors can judge whether it is new spending or substitution.

Indicators to monitor

Four indicators will test this argument: whether Gartner revises its services forecast down again while raising data center systems; Wipro's December quarter results against its guidance; whether Infosys and HCLTech AI revenue begins to lift total growth rather than replace existing work; and the pace at which HyperVault and similar ventures convert commitments into contracted capacity.

The technology budget is not shrinking. On Gartner's forecasts, it is being reallocated toward chips, data centers, and software, and services is set to receive a smaller share of a larger total. For India's IT services industry, waiting for the demand cycle to turn may not be enough. On current forecasts, the providers that grow will be those that position themselves where the new spending is going.
