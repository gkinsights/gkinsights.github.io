Fewer shares, slower revenue: What is driving EPAM's adjusted earnings growth

EPAM Systems raised its 2026 earnings outlook on November 5 while cutting its revenue outlook below the range it gave in February. Its third-quarter results show that most of the gain in non-GAAP earnings per share came from a smaller share count after heavy buybacks, while the business itself grew about 2.5 percent.

by Gaurav Kumar · November 2026

Framework: Earned, Bought, Borrowed

EPAM is a global engineering and IT services provider, with 2025 revenue of $5.457 billion and about 57,350 delivery professionals at the end of September 2026. On the headline numbers, its third quarter of 2026 looked strong. Non-GAAP diluted earnings per share rose 14.0 percent to $3.51, GAAP diluted earnings per share rose 32.5 percent to $2.53, and the company raised its full-year earnings guidance. The chief executive cited momentum in what the company calls AI-native work and the launch of a new AI services line.

Revenue tells a different story. It rose 2.3 percent in the quarter, and EPAM now expects fourth-quarter revenue between a decline of 0.2 percent and growth of 0.9 percent. This review separates EPAM's growth into what the business earned, what acquisitions bought, and what the balance sheet supplied. The argument, which is an interpretation of one quarter's results and the company's guidance, is that the adjusted earnings story is increasingly carried by a shrinking share count rather than by the business.

The earned engine is running at about 2.5 percent, and guidance points lower

Organic constant currency revenue growth, which strips out acquisitions and exchange rates, was 2.5 percent in the third quarter and 3.1 percent for the first nine months. In February, EPAM guided to organic constant currency growth of 3 to 6 percent for 2026. It now expects 2.5 to 2.7 percent, and its fourth-quarter outlook calls for organic growth of 0.5 to 1.6 percent. Its guidance for total revenue growth has moved from a range of 4.5 to 7.5 percent to one of 3.5 to 3.7 percent.

Some of the earnings gain is earned. Non-GAAP operating income rose 7.0 percent, faster than revenue, as the non-GAAP operating margin widened to 16.7 percent from 16.0 percent. GAAP operating income rose 21.4 percent, as the items separating GAAP from non-GAAP operating income narrowed by about $15.4 million, by this review's calculation. Delivery headcount rose 1.2 percent from June. But the results release does not quantify AI-native revenue, so outsiders cannot yet test whether the new AI work is offsetting slower demand elsewhere.

Acquisitions carried 2025, and none are carrying 2026

EPAM's reported revenue grew 15.4 percent in 2025. Of that, 9.2 percentage points came from acquired revenue, alongside organic constant currency growth of 4.9 percent and a 1.3 point lift from currency. In 2026, the inorganic contribution has been zero in both the third quarter and the first nine months, and the third-quarter release announced no acquisitions.

In this review's reading, 2025 was a bought year and 2026 is an earned year, which is why 2026 looks so much weaker on the top line. The difference matters for the earnings story, because, in this review's reading, the gap that acquisitions once filled in per-share growth is now being filled by buybacks.

Fewer shares delivered most of the earnings growth

The share count fell because of buybacks over the past year: $660.6 million in 2025, including $223.5 million in the fourth quarter, and about $489 million in the first nine months of 2026. Non-GAAP net income rose 5.3 percent in the third quarter, from $171.8 million to $180.9 million. Non-GAAP diluted earnings per share rose 14.0 percent because the weighted average diluted share count fell 7.8 percent, from 55.8 million to 51.5 million. Had the share count stayed at last year's level, non-GAAP earnings per share would have been about $3.24 rather than $3.51, by this review's calculation. The GAAP picture is more favorable: at last year's share count, GAAP diluted earnings per share would have been about $2.33, so roughly two-thirds of the $0.62 GAAP increase came from higher profit. The finding in this section applies to the adjusted measure that EPAM leads its guidance with.

The split of the $0.43 increase from $3.08 to $3.51 is this review's calculation: non-GAAP net income divided by the prior-year diluted share count gives the earned portion; the remainder reflects the lower share count. Applying the share count change first gives a split of about $0.17 and $0.26.

Over the year to date, buybacks have run ahead of cash generation. EPAM repurchased about $489 million of shares in the first nine months of 2026, while cash from operations over the same period was $250.1 million, down from $372.0 million a year earlier. Operating cash flow is uneven across quarters: third-quarter cash from operations of $288.8 million comfortably covered that quarter's buybacks of about $80 million. Cash, cash equivalents, and restricted cash fell $331.4 million, or 25.5 percent, to $969.9 million. The company now plans a $500 million accelerated share repurchase in the week of November 9, funded by cash and borrowings under its credit facility, and its board approved a further $1.5 billion of authorization through 2029.

Since February, EPAM's revenue outlook has come down and its earnings outlook has gone up, and the share count explains most of the difference.

The full-year guidance shows the same pattern. EPAM now expects non-GAAP earnings per share of $13.23 to $13.31, up from $12.60 to $12.90 in February and about 15 percent above its 2025 figure of $11.50, by this review's calculation. Over the same period it lowered expected revenue growth and cut its assumed diluted share count for the year from 54.4 million to 52.0 million. Multiplying the guidance midpoints by the assumed share counts gives implied non-GAAP net income of about $694 million in February and about $690 million now, by this review's calculation, so the higher earnings per share outlook rests entirely on fewer shares.

Verdict
In the third quarter, a little under two-fifths of EPAM's non-GAAP earnings per share growth was earned through higher profit, none was bought through acquisitions, and the rest came from a share count reduced by buybacks, which over the first nine months of 2026 ran at nearly twice operating cash flow. On GAAP earnings per share, the earned share is larger, at about two-thirds. Competing explanation: the strongest alternative reading is that this is sound capital allocation through a cyclical slowdown in client spending: EPAM still held $969.9 million of cash, its margins genuinely improved, third-quarter operating cash flow covered that quarter's buybacks, and returning cash at a time of weak demand is a reasonable choice. That reading may be right about the buybacks. It does not change the finding that adjusted earnings per share now overstate the momentum of the business, and one quarter of results cannot separate a cyclical slowdown from a structural one driven by AI productivity. Testable call: when EPAM gives its first 2027 outlook with its fourth-quarter 2026 results (last year's equivalent came on February 19, 2026), the midpoint of its guided 2027 organic constant currency revenue growth will be below 3 percent, beneath the 4.9 percent it delivered in 2025. A midpoint of 3 percent or more would show the earned engine restarting and weaken this review's reading.

What EPAM's leadership could consider

EPAM's leadership could consider three actions.

  1. Quantify AI-native revenue. A disclosed figure with a definition and a growth rate would let investors judge whether new AI work is replacing older engineering demand or adding to it.

  2. Show the earnings bridge. Reporting how much of each period's earnings per share growth comes from operations and how much from the share count would make the quality of growth visible without outside calculation.

  3. Pace buybacks to cash generation. EPAM has committed to return at least 50 percent of free cash flow annually from 2027 to 2029. Keeping repurchases within operating cash generation, rather than adding credit facility borrowings, would preserve flexibility for acquisitions or AI investment if organic growth stays low.

Indicators to monitor
Fourth-quarter 2026 revenue against guidance of a 0.2 percent decline to 0.9 percent growth; the amount drawn on the credit facility for the accelerated repurchase, as disclosed in later filings; full-year 2026 cash from operations against the nine-month decline; the initial 2027 outlook for organic constant currency growth and diluted share count; and any quantified disclosure of AI-native revenue.

EPAM's third quarter can be read as a strong quarter, and on margins and earnings per share it was. The framework shows why both readings coexist. The business earned modest growth, acquisitions contributed nothing, and on the adjusted measure buybacks supplied most of the rest. In this review's view, that mix depends on cash and borrowing capacity rather than on clients, which is why the revenue line, not the earnings line, will decide how EPAM's AI-native strategy is judged.
