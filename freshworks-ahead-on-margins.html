Ahead on margins, behind on expansion: Testing Freshworks' plan against its results

Freshworks passed $1 billion of recurring revenue early and now guides its 2026 operating margin into the range it set for 2028. The growth half of its plan, an employee experience business it describes as a mid-20s grower, grew 22 percent in the third quarter, down from 25 percent in the first.

by Gaurav Kumar · November 2026

Framework: Said, Did, Delivered

Freshworks, which its chief executive describes as the first Indian software-as-a-service company to list on Nasdaq, sells two kinds of products. Its employee experience (EX) business, led by the Freshservice IT service management suite, is the growth engine. Its customer experience (CX) business, led by Freshdesk, is the slower line. On November 3, 2026, the company reported third-quarter revenue of $245.4 million, up 14 percent, a second straight quarter of GAAP profit, and said it had passed $1 billion of annual recurring revenue (ARR) in October, ahead of its year-end goal.

The tension is between two promises made together. At a financial analyst session on May 14, 2026, management raised its 2028 targets on both margins and growth. This review tests that plan by comparing what was said, where resources went, and what the results show. The argument, which is an interpretation of three quarters of results, is that the margin promise is on track to be kept early while the EX growth promise is drifting.

Said: the plan promises both faster EX growth and fatter margins by 2028

On May 14, 2026, Freshworks lifted the 2028 targets it set at its 2025 investor day. Revenue moved to more than $1.3 billion from more than $1.2 billion, the non-GAAP operating margin to 24 to 26 percent from 20 to 22 percent, and stock-based compensation was to fall to 13 to 14 percent of revenue. The growth assumption also rose: EX ARR, previously expected to grow above 20 percent, was now expected to grow in the mid-20s, with Freshservice on a path to about $1 billion of ARR in 2028. Reduced to one testable sentence, the claim is that Freshworks can grow EX in the mid-20s each year to 2028 while expanding margins. The company repeated the growth expectation on its August and November calls.

Did: the money went to efficiency, acquisitions, and buybacks

The resource choices back the margin half of the plan most clearly. In May 2026, Freshworks announced a restructuring to streamline the organization and make more use of AI and automation. On July 1, it consolidated its CX organization in India, where a single go-to-market team now serves a mostly small business customer base. Stock-based compensation, on the basis used in the results release, fell to 15.8 percent of revenue in the third quarter from 22.6 percent a year earlier, by this review's calculation.

Capital went to two places. On growth, Freshworks bought FireHydrant, an incident management company, for $88.7 million in cash on January 1, 2026, and in October bought Torii and Robusta for a preliminary consideration of approximately $102.5 million, which management expects to add $8 million to $10 million of ARR. On shareholder returns, it repurchased $231.7 million of stock in the first nine months of 2026, excluding excise tax, more than its adjusted free cash flow of $187.4 million over the same period. The buybacks were concentrated in the first half: third-quarter repurchases of $27.2 million were well below that quarter's adjusted free cash flow of $73.9 million.

Delivered: margins are reaching the 2028 range early, while EX growth has slowed

On margins, Freshworks is running ahead of schedule. Its full-year 2026 guidance for non-GAAP operating income of $238 million to $240 million on revenue of $965 million to $966 million implies a margin of about 24.8 percent at the midpoints, by this review's calculation, inside the 2028 range. In May, the 2026 guidance for that income was $207 million to $215 million. The result so far is slightly below the range: the non-GAAP operating margin for the first nine months was 23.9 percent, lifted by 29.8 percent in the third quarter.

On EX growth, the record is weaker. As reported, EX ARR grew 22 percent at the end of 2025, 25 percent in the first quarter of 2026, then 23 percent and 22 percent. In constant currency, growth held at 24 percent in the first two quarters and fell to 22 percent in the third. These rates include FireHydrant from January, which the company describes as not material to its results. The chief financial officer said on the November call that the fourth quarter looks like about 22 percent in constant currency. Net dollar retention for EX customers excluding the legacy Device42 base was over 109 percent in the third quarter, against over 111 percent in constant currency in the second.

The CX business, now run from India, has been assigned stability and cash rather than growth. CX ARR grew 4 percent, and management said in May that it wants CX to be a business with an operating margin of about 30 percent. Its customer-facing seat base faces a structural question: a Gartner survey released on November 5 found that 21 percent of service and support leaders have reduced human agent headcount because of AI. Freshworks is building a consumption line in response, and its CX AI Agent Studio added more than $2.5 million of new ARR in the third quarter, still small against a $404 million base.

Freshworks is keeping the promise it controls, cost, and drifting on the one its customers control, expansion.

Verdict
Said and did agree on margins, and did and delivered are converging: cost actions have put 2026 margin guidance inside the 2028 range, though the year to date sits just below it. The mismatch is between said and delivered on EX growth. Management still describes EX as a mid-20s grower while its own fourth-quarter outlook points to about 22 percent in constant currency, with an acquisition included. The verdict is drifting, not theater: the gap is a few points, management has acknowledged the near-term rate, and the margin claim is close to being met. Competing explanation: the slowdown may be a deliberate transition rather than weaker demand. Device42 customers are moving from upfront on-premise licenses to cloud subscriptions, which the company says means less upfront term license revenue going forward, management called the dip in EX retention roughly in line with its expectations, and it says it has not yet switched on its expansion motion with the roughly 70 percent of EX customers using one product. That reading is plausible, and three quarters of data cannot separate the two. Note too that FireHydrant has flattered 2026 growth and will stop doing so once it laps in 2027. Testable call: at its second-quarter 2027 results, Freshworks will report constant currency EX ARR growth below 21 percent, even with Torii and Robusta included. Growth of 21 percent or more would show the expansion motion working and weaken this review's reading.

What Freshworks' leadership could consider

Freshworks' leadership could consider three actions.

  1. Report EX growth without acquisitions. With three deals in a year, an organic EX ARR growth rate would show whether the mid-20s expectation rests on the existing business.

  2. Disclose expansion progress. The share of EX customers using more than one product, reported each quarter, would let investors see the expansion motion that the plan depends on before it shows up in ARR.

  3. Disclose AI consumption revenue. Session-based ARR in CX and EX would show whether consumption pricing can offset pressure on seats as customers automate service work.

Indicators to monitor
EX ARR at year-end 2026 against the company's expectation of more than $625 million, which includes the acquired ARR from Torii and Robusta; EX net dollar retention excluding Device42; any 2027 statement on EX growth given with fourth-quarter results; CX ARR growth and AI consumption revenue; and the pace of share repurchases against adjusted free cash flow.

Freshworks has shown, faster than its own plan required, that it can make money. The question for 2027 is the one its plan treats as settled: whether its installed base of IT buyers will keep expanding at the rate its targets need. The margin story is close to its 2028 destination. The growth story has to be shown again each quarter, and on the latest evidence it is running a little behind the words used to describe it.
