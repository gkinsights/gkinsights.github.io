Analysis of technology markets and the companies competing in them.

Writing

  • The scale threshold: Why India's quick commerce boom may not lift all platforms

    On their latest results, Blinkit is about three times Instamart's size and profitable, while Swiggy says Instamart needs about 2.3 times its current scale to break even.

  • The growth cliff: Why ServiceNow's second doubling will be harder than its first

    ServiceNow's 2030 target implies about 17 percent annual growth, and excluding Armis its forward revenue indicator is already at that pace. The next engines sit in markets where other companies set the terms.

  • Paying for the platform: Four strategic risks in ServiceNow's AI expansion

    A strategic risk review from public filings: acquired growth that is not disclosed separately, a premium paid for Armis, a widening gap between GAAP and non-GAAP margins, and seat-based pricing exposed to AI.

  • The services slice: Why IT services is getting a shrinking share of the AI spending boom

    Gartner expects services to take less than 8 percent of the growth in worldwide IT spending in 2027, and India's largest providers grew more slowly than the services segment in their latest quarter.

  • Agents versus seats: Four strategic risks in Salesforce's AI pivot

    A strategic risk review from public filings: an AI product that can reduce seats, growth excluding acquisitions near 6 percent, a pricing model in transition, and a debt-funded buyback.

  • The AI apocalypse, so far: A valuation event, not yet a revenue event for the largest platforms

    Software stocks fell sharply on AI fears in 2026 while two of the largest platforms kept growing. The more immediate risk lies in how software is priced.

  • The GCC divide: Why not every capability center will make the climb

    Industry data places 56 percent of India's GCCs below the top two maturity stages. AI may make the climb harder for centers built around repeatable work.

  • Robots in a low-wage economy: Where physical AI is likely to pay off in India

    With labor costs a fraction of those in the markets piloting humanoids, the case for robots in India is more likely to rest on quality and export requirements.

  • Revenue without headcount: Rethinking the Indian IT services model

    India's two largest IT services providers signed large deals in FY26 while the five largest together reduced headcount. The shift is likely to outlast the demand cycle.
